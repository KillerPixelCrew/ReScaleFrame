## Question and result

How can AC7's D3D11 renderer use DLSS-G without a second physical swapchain, a second
Streamline instance, or a Present counter masquerading as an input frame?

The experimental cold profile returns a D3D11 facade over one runtime-owned D3D12 DLSS-G
chain. The native SR graph remains the reconstruction owner. Its dense depth/motion and camera
cross to that same host, using one CPU-minted SDK token and one common-constants submission.
Copied source/view/window identity gates interpolation. This is an MVP. AC7 flight, HUD quality
and latency acceptance are pending; a synthetic activation fixture is not a game run.

## Driver latency ring at stall onset, 4 October 11:39

Five stall rings belong to 33333-microsecond stalls (sources 20651, 21538, 27677, 29263 and

System fact not recorded before: this is a hybrid laptop (Acer Predator PH18-71). The
2560x1600 165 Hz panel is driven by Intel UHD Graphics and the RTX 4070 Laptop GPU renders,
so every Present crosses adapters. The game runs windowed at 1600x900

Finding 1. In each of the five stall rings exactly one frame carries an invalid
`crossAdapterCopyTimeUs`: `0x88000000` twice and `0x90000000` three times. It is always the
frame four before the sleep that stalls (20647, 21534, 27673, 29259, 30809). Every other
frame in those rings reports a multiple of about 440 microseconds: 880 for most frames with
generation, occasionally 440 or 1320, and 440 without generation. No baseline frame has an
invalid value. Markers, driver, queue and GPU times of the flagged frame are otherwise
ordinary. The pair of values differs by `0x08000000`, which looks like a status or raw
encoding rather than a measured time; what the driver means by it is not documented in the
NVAPI or Streamline headers read here.

Finding 2. With generation active the driver's frame interval never settles. In all five
rings the interval between SimulationStart markers swings between about 4.8 and 7.1 ms with a
period of some 40 frames. The flagged frame sits at or just after an interval peak in four of
five rings.

Finding 3. With generation active the driver reports the frame's GPU end a median 8.6 to 9.0
ms after PresentEnd, against a 6.3 to 6.6 ms frame interval, and as little as 3.1 to 3.4 ms at
other times. Without generation the same delay is 0.22 ms. Four of the five flagged frames are
in the short state (3.3 to 4.0 ms). The presentation path therefore alternates between
holding roughly one extra frame and holding none, and the invalid sample appears around the
transition.

What this establishes: the stall is the driver reacting to its own invalid cross-adapter copy
sample, four frames later, and that sample occurs while the generated-frame present queue
changes depth. It also confirms the earlier audit: nothing in the marker stream of the
flagged frame or its neighbours is missing, duplicated or out of order.

What it does not establish: why the copy sample becomes invalid. The reading consistent with
all three findings is that about 312 presents per second (two per 6.4 ms source frame) are
being sent through a composed, cross-adapter path that shows 165, so presents queue up and
are periodically superseded, and one such event leaves the driver without a valid copy time.
That is an inference. No per-present data (present mode, discarded presents) was captured.
